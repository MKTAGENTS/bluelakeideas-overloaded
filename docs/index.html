Section 04

Campaign Methodology — Email Sequence Best Practices

The principle

Each email has one job. Activate, deliver, or convert. Never re-pitch.

Repeating the value prop across multiple sends drains attention. The pitch already lives on the landing page and the welcome. Once they're on the list, every email needs a new reason to open.

The honest read on the current draft

The welcome (Section 02) makes the full argument. Launch Email 1 (Section 03) makes the same argument again. That's redundant.

The fix: rewrite Launch Email 1 as pure activation — "It's live. Here's the link. Here's why today." No re-pitch.

Email 2 (Introduction) and Email 3 (Launch Day) each have a distinct job. Those are working.

The 5-role framework

Every email in a launch campaign plays one role. Three are core. Two are optional based on cadence.

1 Welcome — immediate, on signup
Job: Set expectations + deliver one piece of immediate value.
Lands well
A quote, a clip, a 1-pager, a single insight
Doesn't
Re-pitching the book
2 Nurture — optional, during the wait
Job: Stay top of mind. Build authority. Cadence: weekly or bi-weekly.
Lands well
One idea per send, one video, one story
Doesn't
Feature dumps or "ICYMI" digests
3 Announce — day pre-orders open
Job: Activate the moment. Don't re-sell.
Lands well
News + link + one sentence on why today
Doesn't
The case for the book — that's already been made
4 Deliver value — mid-window
Job: Give them something tangible. The Introduction. A bonus chapter. A live AMA invite.
Lands well
The asset + one pull-quote + a low-pressure CTA
Doesn't
Front-loading the ask
5 Final call — launch day or last day of pre-order
Job: Convert the fence-sitters.
Lands well
Urgency, personal voice, last-chance bonuses
Doesn't
Sounding desperate or re-pitching

What Matt and Anne need to provide — by email

For each role, the inputs we need to draft. Send the inputs; we'll handle the structure, voice, and HubSpot setup.

Welcome

  • One "first taste" asset — clip, quote, 1-pager. What should they read or watch immediately?
  • Optional: a preview of what's coming next

Nurture (if running)

  • 3–6 short ideas or stories Matt's been working with
  • Existing videos to highlight
  • Any new podcast or article appearances to share

Announce

  • Pre-order live date
  • Pre-order link(s) — retail + direct
  • One sentence on why this is the moment

Introduction drop

  • The Introduction itself (PDF or web page)
  • One pull-quote
  • The next CTA — continue to pre-order

Launch day

  • Retail link(s)
  • A short personal note from Matt — 5–10 sentences
  • Any launch-day-only bonuses or events

The rules — every send

  • One goal per email. If two compete, split into two emails.
  • One CTA per email. Two CTAs = the reader picks neither.
  • Lead with the news, never the framing. The first sentence does the work.
  • Matt's voice on the critical sends. Launch Day is signed by Matt personally — not the team.
  • Subject line is the headline. Write it last. Edit it twice.
  • Test subject lines. Open rate tells us what works.
Section 05

Driving Organic Traffic — SEO & AEO

The play

Search and AI engines are how leaders find their next book. We'll own both.

Why it matters: Pre-orders aren't promotion. Without organic discoverability, the waitlist runs on Matt's owned channels alone. SEO and AEO turn /overloaded into a long-tail engine that compounds for the life of the book.

The bottom line: Three plays — on-page SEO, topic clusters, and AEO — sequenced over the pre-launch window.

1. On-Page SEO — the /overloaded page

Page metadata

  • Title tag: Overloaded: Recapturing Leadership Capacity with the Power of & | Matt Elliott
  • Meta description: ~155 chars — hook, value, CTA
  • URL slug: /overloaded
  • H1: Overloaded.
  • H2/H3: Why this book exists · What's inside · About Matt Elliott
  • Image alt text: every image describes content with target terms

Schema markup (JSON-LD in header)

  • Book — title, author, publisher, ISBN once assigned
  • Person — Matt as author, with credentials and sameAs links
  • FAQPage — for the AEO section (see below)

2. Target Keywords

Primary (high intent)

leadership capacity the power of and binary thinking leadership capacity advantage

Long-tail (AEO gold)

how to recapture leadership capacity managing tensions in leadership AI era leadership challenges leaders feel overloaded false choices in business

Branded

Matt Elliott book Blue Lake Ideas Overloaded book & Intelligence Framework

3. Topic Cluster — hub & spoke

The hub: /overloaded (the waitlist page).

The spokes: definitional pages, each linking back to /overloaded with the CTA to join the waitlist.

  • /the-power-of-and — definition + the framework's origin
  • /capacity-advantage — concept page with examples
  • /and-intelligence-framework — Pause and Reflect, Find the &, Test and Learn
  • /four-taxes — false choices, silos, short-termism, stalled innovation
  • /video — the BLI YouTube hub (already live)

Each spoke = SEO surface area + AEO answer-engine fodder.

4. AEO — Answer Engine Optimization

What it is

Optimization for AI engines — ChatGPT, Perplexity, Claude, Gemini, Google AI Overviews — that synthesize answers from web content.

Why it matters

Executives increasingly ask AI for book recommendations and frameworks before they Google. AEO determines whether Matt's name surfaces.

Tactics

  • Definitional content: Each framework term gets a single-page, citable definition (~150–250 words). Plain language. AI engines reward clean question-answer structure.
  • FAQ schema on /overloaded — What is the Power of &? Who is Matt Elliott? What is binary thinking? When does Overloaded ship?
  • Author entity: Matt's bio page with Person schema — speaking credits, podcasts, quoted articles. Citable signals.
  • Knowledge Graph: submit to Wikidata, work toward a Wikipedia presence.
  • Indexed citations: guest posts and interviews in HBR, Forbes, Fast Company, Inc., MIT Sloan Review — publications AI engines crawl most.

5. Off-Page Boost

  • Podcasts: every appearance includes /overloaded in show notes
  • YouTube: every video description pinned to /overloaded
  • LinkedIn: Matt's organic posts route to /overloaded
  • Email signature: Matt and the team's sig blocks include the link
  • Guest posts: 3–5 industry pieces over the pre-launch window, each backlinked

6. Tracking — what we measure

  • Squarespace Analytics — page views, time on page, signup conversion rate
  • UTM tags on every external link — ?utm_source=linkedin&utm_medium=post&utm_campaign=overloaded
  • HubSpot lead-source attribution — which channels drive actual signups (see Section 05)
  • Google Search Console — query data, impressions, CTR by keyword
Section 06

Squarespace Capture → HubSpot Email & CRM

The play

Capture in the Squarespace Newsletter Block. Bridge to HubSpot via Zapier. HubSpot owns every email — welcome and launch sequence.

Why HubSpot for the sends: single source of truth. Email engagement (opens, clicks, replies) lives on the contact record, feeding segmentation, lead scoring, and any future sales handoff. Templates, workflows, and A/B testing are stronger than Squarespace Email's native tools.

The architecture

One Newsletter Block submission triggers one path. HubSpot does the rest.

Newsletter Block submit → Zapier → HubSpot Contact + List → HubSpot Workflow → Welcome + Launch Sequence

The setup — 5 steps

  1. Prep HubSpot.
    • Create a static list: Overloaded Waitlist
    • Add custom contact properties: Waitlist Joined Date, UTM Source, Pre-ordered (Y/N), Coaching Interest
    • Verify the sending domain (bluelakeideas.com) is connected and authenticated for marketing email
  2. Build the welcome email + launch sequence in HubSpot.
    • Marketing → Email → create 4 emails using the copy from Sections 02 and 03
    • Apply brand template (Deep Blue Lake header, Libre Baskerville + DM Sans, & circle as sender avatar, Power of & horizontal in signature)
    • Set sender: Blue Lake Ideas <hello@bluelakeideas.com> for emails 1–3, Matt Elliott <matt@bluelakeideas.com> for launch day
  3. Build the welcome workflow.
    • Automation → Workflows → Contact-based workflow
    • Enrollment trigger: Contact added to "Overloaded Waitlist" list
    • Action 1: Send welcome email (immediate, no delay)
    • Mark workflow as active
  4. Connect Squarespace → Zapier.
    • Newsletter Block → Storage tab → Connect to Zapier
    • Squarespace API key: Settings → Advanced → Squarespace API Keys → scope: Forms
    • Zap: Squarespace New Form Submission → HubSpot Create or Update Contact (map First Name, Email, Lead Source, Waitlist Joined Date) → HubSpot Add Contact to Static List (Overloaded Waitlist)
    • The list-add triggers the welcome workflow automatically — no second zap needed
  5. Schedule the 3-email launch sequence as marketing email sends.
    • Email 1 (announce pre-orders) — scheduled for pre-order open day, sent to the Overloaded Waitlist list
    • Email 2 (Introduction) — scheduled 3–5 days later
    • Email 3 (launch day) — scheduled for launch day, sent from Matt's address
    • Each send filtered by list = Overloaded Waitlist

What each tool handles

Squarespace

  • The landing page (/overloaded)
  • Newsletter Block capture only — no sends from Squarespace

Zapier

  • One zap. Submission → HubSpot contact + list-add
  • UTM data and Waitlist Joined Date set as custom properties at signup

HubSpot — everything else

  • Welcome workflow triggered by list membership — sends within seconds
  • 3-email launch sequence as scheduled marketing emails to the full list
  • Engagement tracking on each contact (opens, clicks, replies)
  • Segmentation — by source, engagement, future pre-order status, coaching interest
  • Sales handoff — if a waitlist member becomes a coaching or speaking inquiry later, HubSpot has full history. No relationship starts cold

Plan & cost

  • Squarespace: Core plan or higher (required for Zapier integration)
  • Zapier: Starter at $30/mo — needed for multi-step Zaps
  • HubSpot: Marketing Hub Starter at $20/mo minimum — required for marketing email sends and workflows. Free CRM alone won't send emails
  • Domain authentication: SPF, DKIM, DMARC records added for bluelakeideas.com so HubSpot sends from the brand domain with full deliverability

Why HubSpot for sends beats Squarespace Email here

  • One source of truth. Engagement and contact data live in the same place. No reconciling lists across two platforms.
  • Workflows automate the welcome. List-add fires the send instantly. No manual scheduling for welcome emails.
  • Better segmentation downstream. Opens, clicks, and replies update the contact record automatically — so we can target the next campaign by who engaged.
  • Cleaner sales handoff. When a waitlist member becomes a coaching or speaking lead, the full email history is already attached to their record in HubSpot.
  • Template control. One brand template applied across all sends, including the email signature with Matt's social links.
